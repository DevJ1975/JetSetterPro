# Travel etiquette: Japan

## Greetings and cards
A slight bow is the customary greeting. When exchanging business cards (meishi), offer and receive
with both hands and take a moment to read the card before putting it away respectfully.

## Shoes and indoor spaces
Remove your shoes when entering homes, traditional inns (ryokan), temples, and some restaurants. Look
for a step-up entry (genkan) and slippers provided for indoor use; use separate slippers for the
toilet.

## Public conduct
Keep phone calls and loud conversation to a minimum on trains. Eating while walking is generally
avoided. Form orderly lines and let passengers exit before boarding.

## Money and tipping
Tipping is not customary and can cause confusion; excellent service is the standard. Cash is still
widely used — carry some yen, and place money on the small tray at registers rather than handing it
directly.

## Onsen basics
Wash and rinse thoroughly before entering a hot-spring bath. Bathing is done without swimwear, and
tattoos may be restricted at some facilities.
